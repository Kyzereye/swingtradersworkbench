## Definitions

**Trend-intact segment** — a maximal run of bars with close above the slow MA and fast above
slow. A decline outside one of these is a downtrend, not a pullback.

**Pullback episode** — inside a segment, price trading below the running peak of `high`. The
episode ends on a new high (**recovered**) or when the segment breaks (**break**). Depth is
peak → trough, in percent and in ATR units at the peak bar. Dips shallower than the noise floor
are discarded, but the peak is **not** reset when one is — so a shallow dip that deepens without
an intervening new high is one deep episode, not zero.

**Depth pools, pierce rate splits.** The two conventions differ on purpose:

- Depth statistics **include break episodes**. Segment-ending dips are often the deep ones and
  cluster in exactly the choppy names this metric exists to identify — excluding them would tend
  to make a symbol look *shallower* the worse it actually behaves. They are not *always* deep:
  when fast and slow sit close together a segment can break on a shallow wobble, which is why
  this is a reporting split and not an assertable invariant.
- The headline pierce rate uses **recovered episodes only**. A break by close ≤ slow while
  fast > slow implies close < slow < fast, so break episodes are pierced *by construction*;
  pooling them would manufacture a pierce rate that only tracks how often trends end. The
  all-episode rate is shown beside it so the gap is visible.

**Unresolved episodes** — one open at the window edge has no final trough, so it is not recorded.
That drops in the same direction as excluding breaks would, so the count is reported per symbol
rather than dropped silently.

## Persistence

Spearman rho between each symbol's first-half and second-half measurement, across symbols.
Both halves cover the same number of measurable bars; the MAs and ATR still warm up on full
history, so the second half sees the values a full-history run would.

**This is the gating result.** A tendency that does not repeat cannot rank or filter anything,
no matter how well it correlates in-sample.

## Conclusion

**Persistence gate (rho >= 0.3 between the two halves).** Passed by: Median pullback depth % (+0.577), Bar pierce rate % (+0.744), Episode pierce rate % (recovered) (+0.434). Failed by: Median depth (ATR units) (+0.155), Break share % of episodes (+0.068) — treat those as noise regardless of what the quintile tables show.

**Is it just volatility?** Median pullback depth vs median ATR%: +0.879 (n=496). The closer that sits to +1, the more the raw depth metric is ATR wearing a different hat, and the more the within-ATR-quintile tables are the only ones worth reading.

**Tautology check.** Bar pierce rate vs trade count: +0.445 (n=496). Pierce rate is close to "how often the exit rule fires", so a high correlation here means the metric is largely restating trade count rather than adding information.

**Median pullback depth %** vs return: optimized +0.281, fixed 21/50 +0.106. Both columns agree in sign, so the relationship is not purely an artefact of the per-symbol MA fit.

**Median depth (ATR units)** vs return: optimized -0.008, fixed 21/50 +0.055. **The sign flips between the two return columns** — the optimized relationship is a grid-search artefact, not a property of the symbol.

**Bar pierce rate %** vs return: optimized -0.059, fixed 21/50 -0.104. Both columns agree in sign, so the relationship is not purely an artefact of the per-symbol MA fit.

**Episode pierce rate % (recovered)** vs return: optimized -0.050, fixed 21/50 -0.085. Both columns agree in sign, so the relationship is not purely an artefact of the per-symbol MA fit.

**Break share % of episodes** vs return: optimized -0.454, fixed 21/50 -0.477. Both columns agree in sign, so the relationship is not purely an artefact of the per-symbol MA fit.

**Every tendency bucket has a positive median return.** That is the same shape every prior gate ran into (docs/improvement-options.md §1): blocking a profitable bucket deletes expectancy. If this metric is used at all, it is a ranking key under scarce slots, not a filter.

Median is the headline number because a single outlier symbol can dominate the mean, and Spearman is used throughout for the same reason.

## Break-depth notes

below 60% of the recovered median). Informational, not a
problem: it means the trend tends to end on a small wobble rather than a deep dip, which is what
a fast MA sitting close to the slow one produces.
